Seaspan Shipyards · Workforce Planning

Supervisor Workforce Validation

Developed a discipline-specific workforce analysis to evaluate whether planned supervisory staffing levels were consistent with historical production behavior and future rebaseline requirements.

Client Program Management Office
Analysis Supervisor-to-Production Ratios
Disciplines 6 Major Trades
Outcome Staffing Recommendations

01 · The Question

Were Future Staffing Assumptions Realistic?

During the JSS2 rebaseline, PMO requested an independent review of planned supervisory staffing.

The goal was to determine whether the number of supervisors planned for future production was consistent with the amount of blue-collar work expected within each discipline.

Rather than applying one project-wide staffing ratio, I developed discipline-specific benchmarks because the supervision required for different trades can vary significantly.

02 · Building the Method

Using Historical Labor Behavior as a Benchmark

Historical Production Labor

Reviewed recent blue-collar labor consumption within each discipline to establish the production workload being supported.

Historical Supervisory Labor

Compared supervisor labor over the same periods to understand how supervisory demand had historically scaled with production.

Discipline-Specific Ratios

Created separate historical supervisor-to-production ratios rather than assuming that Electrical, Steel, Mechanical, Paint, and other trades should behave identically.

Future Rebaseline Plan

Compared historical relationships with the time-phased supervisory and blue-collar labor planned in the proposed rebaseline.

Trend Validation

Looked beyond a single average by considering recent labor behavior and whether future staffing patterns made operational sense.

Recommendation Development

Used the differences between historical and planned ratios to identify disciplines that were appropriately staffed, potentially understated, or potentially overstated.

03 · Scope

Six Production Disciplines

Electrical Piping Mechanical Paint Services Steel

The analysis showed that a single staffing assumption would hide important differences between disciplines.

Some planned ratios aligned closely with historical behavior, while others suggested potential over- or under-allocation of supervisory effort.

04 · Findings

Turning Ratios Into Actionable Planning Insight

Generally Aligned

Piping and Electrical staffing assumptions were broadly consistent with the historical relationships observed in the available project data.

Potentially Understated

Mechanical and Paint showed indications that planned supervisory effort may have been lower than historical workload relationships would suggest.

Potentially Overstated

Services and Steel showed indications that some planned supervisory effort may have been higher than historical ratios implied.

05 · Transferable Skills & Takeaways

What Made the Analysis Valuable

Industrial Engineering Thinking

The assignment required connecting labor demand, production workload, staffing structure, and future planning rather than treating workforce numbers as purely financial data.

Benchmark Development

I had to decide what historical relationship would provide a meaningful comparison and build the methodology in a way that remained understandable to stakeholders reviewing the result.

Data Interpretation

A ratio outside the historical range did not automatically mean the plan was wrong. The result had to be interpreted against future scope, production conditions, discipline behavior, and operational context.

Scenario Evaluation

The analysis helped identify where labor could potentially be redistributed between disciplines to improve alignment with expected project needs without simply increasing total hours.

Direct PMO Communication

I presented the methodology, historical benchmarks, findings, concerns, and possible corrective actions directly to PMO, requiring the analysis to be both defensible and easy to explain.

Decision Support

The objective was not to produce a chart for its own sake. The analysis was designed to help management decide whether future staffing assumptions should remain unchanged or be adjusted before the rebaseline was finalized.

06 · Outcome

Moving From Analysis to Recommendation

Future Figure: Sanitized Historical vs Planned Staffing Ratios

The final output provided PMO with a discipline-by-discipline comparison between historical staffing behavior and the proposed future workforce plan.

Rather than only identifying differences, I also highlighted where adjustments appeared most justified and discussed potential redistribution of supervisory hours between disciplines.

This was one of the assignments that most clearly connected my project-controls role with operations and industrial engineering, because the analysis directly addressed how people should be allocated to support future production.
