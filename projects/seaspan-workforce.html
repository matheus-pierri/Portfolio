Seaspan Shipyards · Workforce Planning

Supervisor Workforce Validation

Developed a discipline-level workforce analysis comparing historical supervisor-to-production labor relationships against future rebaseline staffing plans to identify areas that may have been over- or under-planned.

Disciplines Analyzed 6
Comparison Historical vs Future
Focus Supervisor Staffing
Audience PMO

01 · The Question

Does Future Supervision Match the Work Being Planned?

During the rebaseline, future production and support staffing profiles were being rebuilt across multiple shipbuilding disciplines.

One planning question was whether the amount of supervisory labor being forecast remained reasonable relative to the production workforce it would support.

Rather than evaluating supervisor hours in isolation, I developed an analysis comparing historical supervisor-to-production labor relationships against the future staffing profile.

02 · Analytical Approach

Using Historical Execution as a Planning Reference

Separate by Discipline

The analysis was performed independently across six production disciplines rather than assuming one supervision rate would apply equally across all work.

Establish Historical Behavior

Historical supervisor and production labor were compared to identify the staffing relationship each discipline had actually operated with.

Compare Future Plans

The historical relationship was then compared against the future rebaseline profile to identify significant deviations.

Investigate the Difference

Variance from history was treated as a signal to investigate, not automatic proof that the future plan was incorrect.

03 · Example Analysis

Supervisor Rate Over Time

The charted view made it much easier to distinguish normal historical variation from a meaningful change in the planned staffing relationship.

For each discipline, I reviewed how the historical supervision rate behaved over time and compared that range with what the future plan implied.

This provided a simple but effective check on whether the rebaseline was preserving a staffing relationship similar to prior execution or introducing a materially different assumption.

04 · Findings

The Result Was Different by Discipline

Generally Aligned

Piping and Electrical appeared broadly consistent with their historical supervisor-to-production relationships.

Potentially Under-Planned

Mechanical and Paint showed indications that future supervisory requirements may have been understated relative to historical execution.

Potentially Over-Planned

Services and Steel showed indications that future supervisory requirements may have been higher than historical behavior would suggest.

These comparisons were used as planning indicators rather than rigid rules. Differences still required operational context before a recommendation could be made.

05 · Decision Support

Turning the Comparison Into Actionable Recommendations

Once the discipline-level differences were identified, I summarized the results and developed recommendations for areas that warranted further review.

The analysis was presented to PMO, where the discussion could incorporate additional operational context such as changes in work mix, organization, production strategy, and upcoming project phases.

The value of the analysis was therefore not in prescribing one universal staffing ratio. It provided a quantitative reference point that helped challenge whether future workforce assumptions were reasonable.

06 · Industrial Engineering Perspective

Linking Workforce Structure to Production Demand

Ratio Analysis

Converted raw labor data into a normalized relationship that could be meaningfully compared across time.

Capacity Planning

Connected supervisory requirements to the amount of production labor expected to be active in future periods.

Historical Benchmarking

Used actual execution as a benchmark while recognizing that future operating conditions may legitimately differ.

Exception-Based Review

Focused attention on the disciplines showing the largest departure from historical behavior rather than reviewing every result with equal intensity.

07 · Transferable Skills & Takeaways

What This Analysis Developed

Normalize Before Comparing

Raw labor totals can be misleading when workload changes. Ratios created a more useful basis for comparing staffing behavior across time.

Use Data to Ask Better Questions

A deviation from history was not automatically an error. The analysis helped identify where operational explanations or planning changes deserved closer attention.

Present Analysis for Decisions

The final output had to reduce six discipline-level analyses into clear findings that PMO could review and act on.
