01 · Internship Overview

Connecting Project Data to Real Execution

I joined Seaspan within Finance — Program Controls, supporting the Joint Support Ship program across earned value, forecasting, baseline management, reporting, workforce planning, and project analysis.

Although the role sits within Finance, much of the work is closely tied to engineering and production execution. Schedule, cost, resources, progress, and workforce information have to be interpreted together before project performance can be understood.

As the internship progressed, I moved from learning established reporting cycles and Deltek Cobra processes into greater ownership of forecasting methodologies, PMB changes, PMO-requested analyses, major rebaseline work, executive reporting, and process improvement.

One of the biggest lessons has been that strong project controls is not simply reporting CPI, SPI, or EAC. The more important task is understanding whether those metrics accurately describe what is happening in the project — and what management should do next.

04 · What I Learned

Project Controls as Decision Support

Question the Numbers

A report can be mathematically correct and still create a misleading picture if the source data, timing, baseline, or reporting process is not understood.

Understand the Operation

Forecasting becomes much stronger when schedules, production behavior, workforce needs, remaining scope, and account maturity are considered together.

Communicate the Decision

Analysis only creates value when its conclusion can be communicated appropriately to account managers, PMO, directors, and executives.
