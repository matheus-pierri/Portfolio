01 · Internship Overview

Connecting Project Data to Real Execution

I joined Seaspan Shipyards within Finance — Program Controls, supporting the Joint Support Ship program across earned value, forecasting, baseline management, reporting, and project analysis.

Although the position sits within Finance, much of the work is closely tied to engineering and production execution. Schedule, resource, cost, progress, and workforce information must be interpreted together to understand what is actually happening within the project.

As the internship progressed, I moved from learning Deltek Cobra, earned value processes, reporting cycles, and data-quality checks into greater ownership of recurring analyses, baseline changes, forecasting methodologies, PMO studies, executive reporting, and process-improvement work.

The experience has taught me that strong project controls is not simply about calculating CPI, SPI, or EAC. The more important question is whether those numbers accurately describe the work being performed and what management should do next.

04 · What I Learned

Project Controls as Decision Support

Question the Numbers

A metric can be mathematically correct while still giving a misleading picture of performance. Understanding the source and timing of the data is essential before drawing conclusions.

Understand the Operation

Forecasting becomes much more useful when schedule logic, production sequence, workforce behavior, remaining quantities, and account maturity are considered together.

Communicate the Decision

Analysis only creates value when the conclusion can be explained clearly to account managers, PMO, directors, and executives with different levels of technical detail.
