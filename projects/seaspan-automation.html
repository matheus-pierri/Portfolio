Seaspan Shipyards · Process Improvement

Reporting Process Automation

Independently identified a repetitive reporting workflow, taught myself Microsoft Power Automate, and developed an automated SharePoint file-collection process to reduce recurring manual work.

Initiative Self-Directed
Initial Saving ≈ 30 min / week
Broader Potential ≈ 2 h / week
Tools Power Automate · SharePoint

01 · Opportunity

A Small Repetitive Task Repeated Every Week

One recurring reporting process required manually collecting files from SharePoint and moving them into the locations used for weekly reporting.

The task itself was simple, but it was repetitive, predictable, and consumed time every reporting cycle — exactly the type of work that could potentially be automated.

Automating it was not part of my assigned responsibilities. I explored the opportunity independently during available downtime and began learning Microsoft Power Automate specifically to build a solution.

02 · Automated Workflow

Replacing Manual Collection With a Repeatable Flow

I built the workflow incrementally, learning the required Power Automate actions while testing how SharePoint files, folders, conditions, and automated triggers behaved.

The completed flow automated the recurring collection process and reduced approximately 30 minutes of manual work from the initial weekly workflow.

More importantly, the same basic approach appeared applicable to several similar reporting processes, creating a broader opportunity to remove approximately two hours of repetitive weekly work if expanded further.

I treated that broader value as a scale opportunity rather than claiming savings that had not yet been fully implemented.

03 · Development Process

Learning a New Tool Around a Real Problem

Identify the Pattern

Recognized that the task followed the same predictable sequence every reporting cycle and did not require significant human judgment.

Learn & Prototype

Taught myself the required Power Automate functionality and tested the workflow in small pieces before connecting the full process.

Validate & Expand

Confirmed the automated result against the manual process, then identified similar workflows where the same concept could potentially be reused.

04 · Why It Mattered

Small Improvements Become Valuable When They Repeat

Saving 30 minutes once would not be particularly meaningful. Saving it every reporting cycle, while also reducing repetitive manual handling and creating a reusable workflow, made the improvement worthwhile.

The project also reinforced that process improvement does not always need to begin with a large formal initiative. Sometimes the most practical opportunities come from noticing friction in everyday work and being willing to learn the tool needed to remove it.

05 · Transferable Skills & Takeaways

Self-Directed Learning

Learned a new automation platform independently because it could solve an observed workplace problem.

Process Improvement

Looked beyond completing the task efficiently and questioned whether the task needed to be performed manually at all.

Automation Thinking

Broke the manual workflow into triggers, actions, conditions, and repeatable logic that could be translated into automation.

Scalability

Designed the initial solution with similar reporting processes in mind rather than treating it as a one-off script.
