UBC Solar · Vehicle Dynamics

Front Wheel Hub Design & Validation

End-to-end mechanical design of a custom lightweight wheel hub assembly for UBC Solar's next-generation solar race car, integrating wheel, brake, bearing, upright, and retention interfaces.

01 · Overview

Designing a Critical Rotating Assembly

I have owned the mechanical development of the wheel hub system for UBC Solar's Cascadia vehicle, progressing from early concept development and benchmarking through detailed design, engineering validation, manufacturing documentation, and assembly planning.

The wheel hub acts as the central interface between the wheel, braking system, bearings, and suspension upright. This creates a tightly coupled design problem in which structural performance, mass, packaging, manufacturability, tolerances, serviceability, and component compatibility must all be considered together.

A major redesign became necessary after changes to the vehicle's wheel, brake rotor, and caliper architecture. Rather than adapting the previous spindle-based design, I developed a new live-hub configuration in which the hub itself serves as the rotating shaft through the bearing assembly.

02 · Engineering Challenge

Redesigning Around New Vehicle Interfaces

Packaging

Maintained clearance between the wheel, brake rotor, caliper, upright, suspension geometry, and retention hardware while minimizing overall axial length.

Structural Performance

Designed for combined bump, braking, and cornering loads while controlling deflection, fatigue performance, bearing loading, and local stress concentrations.

Manufacturing & Assembly

Balanced precision bearing fits, CNC manufacturability, thermal assembly requirements, serviceability, sealing, and reliable mechanical retention.

03 · Final Architecture

Live Hub with Back-to-Back Tapered Roller Bearings

Wheel Hub Cross-Section / Exploded View

The final architecture uses a custom 6061-T6 aluminum hub supported by two SKF 33206 tapered roller bearings arranged back-to-back in an O configuration.

The arrangement allows the bearing pair to support combined radial and axial wheel loads while providing high moment stiffness. A central upright shoulder locates the bearing outer rings, while the rotating hub interfaces directly with the bearing inner rings.

The brake rotor mounts directly to an integrated hub shoulder using four bolted fasteners. The wheel is secured through a separate bolted locking-plate arrangement, allowing the hub geometry to distribute loads while maintaining removable interfaces for vehicle servicing.

Axial bearing retention is provided through a stepped washer, castle nut, and cotter pin. V-ring seals protect both exposed bearing faces from contamination.

04 · Bearing System Engineering

From Bearing Selection to Preload, Fits, and Life

SKF 33206 Selection

Evaluated bearing architectures and selected a tapered roller bearing pair capable of reacting combined radial and axial vehicle loads within the available package.

Load Analysis

Developed free-body diagrams and force/moment equations to estimate bearing reactions under the combined design loading cases for both front and rear hub configurations.

Preload

Estimated an approximately 2.2 kN bearing preload target and incorporated the requirement into the hub retention system and structural validation.

Fits & Thermal Effects

Evaluated bearing tolerances, radial loading, aluminum-steel thermal expansion, and operating temperature to select interference-fit targets for the hub and upright.

SKF SimPro Validation

Built SKF SimPro Quick models to validate bearing reactions, contact conditions, preload behaviour, safety factors, and predicted bearing life.

Lubrication & Sealing

Compared grease options based on friction and predicted life, selecting SKF LGWM2 alongside V-ring seals to reduce losses and protect the open bearing assembly.

ANSYS Structural Analysis

I developed static and fatigue finite-element models representing the wheel load path through the hub and bearing interfaces. Combined bump, braking, and cornering forces were applied at the tire contact patch and transferred into the hub assembly.

Results were used iteratively rather than solely for final verification. Low-stress regions were identified for mass reduction, while geometry around highly loaded fillets, bearing seats, and interfaces was adjusted to improve structural performance.

Bearing Misalignment

To evaluate whether elastic deformation of the hub and upright could exceed the tapered roller bearing's allowable angular misalignment, I represented bearing and upright compliance using elastic supports in ANSYS.

The resulting worst-case bearing-seat displacement corresponded to approximately 1.18 arcminutes of angular misalignment, comfortably below the approximately 4 arcminute allowable limit.

06 · Machine Design

Validating the Interfaces Around the Hub

Rotor Fasteners

Applied Shigley's bolted-joint methods to size preload and verify no-slip, static, and fatigue safety of the brake rotor connection under combined vehicle loading.

Wheel Interface

Evaluated wheel-retention fasteners and frictional load transfer to ensure the bolted interface could safely transmit braking, cornering, and bump loads.

Threaded Retention

Checked the bearing retention system against thread shear, pull-out, preload, and external axial loading to ensure reliable mechanical locking.

Thermal Assembly

Calculated differential thermal expansion between aluminum components and steel bearing rings to confirm that precision interference fits could be assembled without damaging parts.

07 · Manufacturing Readiness

Taking the Design Beyond CAD

Final Manufacturing Drawing

Engineering Drawing

I produced the front wheel hub manufacturing drawing in accordance with ASME Y14.5 conventions, specifying critical bearing seats, fits, geometric tolerances, threads, bolt patterns, material, and general machining requirements.

Particular attention was given to the bearing interfaces because small dimensional errors directly affect preload, assembly behaviour, bearing life, and rotating alignment.

Assembly Buildbook

I also developed a detailed assembly buildbook defining the parts, tools, safety requirements, bearing preparation, lubrication, thermal-fit sequence, rotor installation, sealing, retention hardware, and final wheel installation.

Developing the assembly procedure forced the design to be evaluated from the perspective of the person actually building and servicing the vehicle, exposing practical constraints that are easy to overlook during CAD development.

08 · Design Process

What the Project Taught Me

Systems Thinking

A wheel hub cannot be optimized independently. Changes to wheels, brakes, bearings, suspension, manufacturing, or assembly propagate through the entire design.

Engineering Iteration

The strongest design decisions came from repeatedly moving between CAD, calculations, simulation, supplier data, packaging constraints, and team feedback.

Design for Reality

A technically valid CAD model is only part of a successful design. Tolerances, manufacturing capability, assembly sequence, serviceability, documentation, and component availability matter just as much.
