01 · Project Overview

Building an Integrated Electromechanical System

This project combined mechanical design, additive manufacturing, electronics integration, embedded programming, sensing, and wireless communication into a single mobile robotic platform.

The robot used differential drive and onboard sensors to autonomously detect and follow a black line, while a separate Bluetooth Low Energy control mode allowed wireless manual operation.

Our team designed and assembled the physical platform ourselves, including the 3D-printed chassis and wheels, battery integration, fastened mechanical interfaces, motors, sensors, and soldered electronics.

The project required the mechanical, electrical, and software subsystems to work reliably together. Problems could not be solved in isolation because sensor placement, chassis geometry, motor response, wiring, control logic, and communication behaviour all affected system performance.

03 · Mechanical Design & Fabrication

Designing the Physical Platform

Chassis CAD / 3D Printing / Assembly Photo

The robot chassis and wheels were produced through additive manufacturing, allowing the mechanical layout to be designed specifically around the required electronics and drive components.

Component packaging had to account for motor position, wheel alignment, sensor placement, battery access, wire routing, fastener locations, and sufficient structural rigidity without unnecessarily increasing the size of the robot.

The final assembly used mechanical fasteners to secure the batteries, electronics, and other components while keeping the system accessible for troubleshooting and modification during testing.

04 · Electronics Integration

From Components to a Working Robot

Soldering / Electronics Board / Wiring

The electrical system was assembled and soldered by the team, combining the Arduino controller, motor connections, sensors, power system, and supporting electronics.

Wiring and component layout became an important part of the mechanical design because the robot needed to remain compact while allowing reliable connections and access for debugging.

Integration testing was performed progressively rather than waiting until the full robot was assembled. Individual sensors, motors, wireless communication, and control functions were checked before being combined into the final system.

05 · Autonomous Line Following

Using Sensor Feedback for Closed-Loop Motion

In autonomous mode, the robot used line-sensor information to determine its position relative to a black path and continuously adjust the left and right motor commands.

Differential-drive control allowed the robot to correct its heading by changing the relative speeds of the two drive motors, enabling it to follow curves and recover from small path errors.

Achieving repeatable behaviour required tuning the relationship between sensor response and motor actuation rather than simply detecting whether a line was present.

06 · Bluetooth Low Energy Control

Adding a Wireless Manual-Control Mode

BLE Remote / Robot Communication Diagram

In addition to autonomous operation, the robot incorporated Bluetooth Low Energy communication to provide a separate wireless control mode.

Commands were transmitted to the robot and converted into differential motor behaviour, allowing forward motion, steering, and stopping without a wired connection.

Implementing both autonomous and manual modes required the embedded program to manage distinct operating states while keeping motor response and communication reliable.

07 · Sensor & Communication Development

Expanding the Robot Beyond Basic Motion

Motion Sensing

Worked with onboard inertial sensing to interpret motion and orientation data as part of the robot's broader sensing system.

Wireless Data Transfer

Used BLE characteristics to communicate control and sensor information between devices rather than relying solely on one-way remote commands.

Sensor Integration

Combined multiple sensing functions within the same embedded platform while managing timing, communication, and control tasks.

08 · Testing & Debugging

Making the Complete System Work Reliably

Robot Testing / Development Photo

The most important challenge was system integration. A mechanical, electrical, or software issue could produce similar symptoms, making structured troubleshooting necessary.

Testing therefore progressed from individual components to complete functions: motor response, sensors, BLE communication, autonomous control, and finally full-robot operation.

The robot was iteratively debugged and tuned until it could repeatedly follow the intended black-line path and respond reliably to wireless control inputs.

10 · Engineering Lessons

What Made the Project Valuable

Integration Matters

A strong mechanical, electrical, or software subsystem is not enough by itself; the overall product only works when all three interact predictably.

Debug Systematically

Breaking problems into individual sensors, motors, communication, and control functions made faults much easier to isolate.

Build, Test, Adjust

Physical testing quickly exposed behaviours that were difficult to anticipate from code or CAD alone, particularly in steering response and sensor-driven control.
