01 · Why PMBCRs Matter

Protecting the Integrity of the Project Baseline

A PMBCR is much more than a budget adjustment. Changes can affect scope, resources, labor distribution, earned-value structure, organizational responsibility, forecasting, and future reporting.

The goal is to allow the project to adapt while keeping the Performance Measurement Baseline accurate and controlled.

04 · Why It Was Challenging

Managing Detail Across an Interconnected System

Requests often arrived with incomplete information, requiring multiple conversations with account managers before the actual intent of the change was clear.

Once technically complete, a package still had to move through a formal approval chain involving groups such as Account Management, Project Controls, Finance, Planning, PMO, and program leadership.

The challenge was therefore not just performing the analysis, but keeping the change accurate, traceable, approved, and consistent with future reporting.

05 · Transferable Skills & Takeaways

What This Work Developed

Requirements Gathering

I learned to recognize when a request did not contain enough information to proceed and to ask targeted questions before making changes. This became important because an incorrect assumption could propagate into budgets, resource plans, forecasts, and performance reporting.

Change & Configuration Control

PMBCR work gave me direct experience with controlled change in a large technical program. Every adjustment needed a clear rationale, defined impacts, supporting documentation, formal approval, and traceability after implementation.

Stakeholder Coordination

Progress often depended on people from several functions. I became responsible for following up on approvals, resolving questions, communicating status, and keeping changes moving without losing sight of the technical requirement.

Attention to Detail at Scale

The work ranged from tiny changes to packages involving roughly 500 activities. That required balancing detailed activity-level accuracy with an understanding of the broader purpose and downstream impact of the change.

Systems Thinking

One of the biggest lessons was seeing how connected project systems are. A baseline change can affect earned value, resource loading, forecasting, account reporting, and management interpretation even when the original request appears relatively small.

Learning the Program Quickly

Because PMBCRs touched many different accounts, they became one of the fastest ways for me to understand how production, engineering, planning, finance, and project controls interact across a major shipbuilding program.

06 · Impact

Supporting Reliable Performance Measurement

By the midpoint of the internship, I had supported much of the PMBCR workflow across approximately 40 changes. The experience gave me practical exposure to baseline governance, configuration control, stakeholder management, and the way approved changes propagate through an earned-value system.

Just as importantly, calculating the post-approval reporting effects allowed the changes to be separated from true operational performance in subsequent variance analysis.
