01 · Why PMBCRs Matter

Controlling Change Without Losing Baseline Integrity

A Performance Measurement Baseline Change Request is more than a budget adjustment. A seemingly small change can affect scope, activity structure, resources, earned value, forecasting, organizational responsibility, and future reporting.

The objective is to let the project adapt while keeping the baseline accurate, traceable, approved, and consistent with the way future performance will be measured.

03 · Typical Workflow

Turning a Change Request Into a Controlled Baseline Update

Requests often arrived with incomplete or ambiguous information, so the first step was frequently understanding what change was actually required and which activities, budgets, resources, or responsibilities would be affected.

From there, I developed or supported the change package, coordinated questions and approvals, tracked outstanding actions, and helped ensure the final change remained consistent with the intended project requirement.

After implementation, I also reviewed the resulting earned-value effect so baseline movement was not later misinterpreted as a change in operational performance.

04 · Why It Was Challenging

Managing Detail Inside an Interconnected System

Incomplete Requirements

I often had to identify missing information and ask targeted questions before the change could be developed correctly.

Cross-Functional Approval

Packages moved through stakeholders across Account Management, Project Controls, Finance, Planning, PMO, and program leadership.

Downstream Effects

A local baseline change could affect resource loading, forecasting, earned value, reporting, and management interpretation elsewhere in the system.

05 · Transferable Skills & Takeaways

What This Work Developed

Requirements Gathering

Learned to recognize when a request was not sufficiently defined and to clarify assumptions before making changes that could propagate through the project system.

Change & Configuration Control

Developed practical experience with controlled change: clear rationale, defined impact, supporting documentation, approval, implementation, and traceability.

Stakeholder Coordination

Kept packages moving by following up on approvals, resolving questions, communicating status, and coordinating between multiple functional groups.

Attention to Detail at Scale

Managed changes ranging from only a few activities to packages affecting roughly 500, requiring both activity-level accuracy and awareness of the broader intent.

Systems Thinking

PMBCR work made the interconnected nature of project systems very clear: cost, schedule, resources, EV, forecasting, and reporting rarely change independently.

Ownership & Organization

Managing many changes simultaneously required consistent tracking, prioritization, follow-up, and documentation so individual packages did not lose momentum.

06 · Impact

Supporting Reliable Performance Measurement

By the midpoint of the internship, I had supported much of the PMBCR lifecycle across approximately 40 changes, giving me practical experience with baseline governance, configuration control, stakeholder management, and project-system integration.

Just as importantly, understanding the reporting effect of each approved change helped separate baseline movement from true operational performance in later variance analysis.
