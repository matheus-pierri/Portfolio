The strongest measure of the project's success was not the complexity of the spreadsheet itself, but what happened after the original analysis was completed.

The methodology was initially developed for a limited JSS2 SOC2 scope. As the approach was reviewed, challenged, refined, and gained confidence from Project Controls and PMO, it was expanded to JSS2 SOC3 and then applied across the equivalent SOC2 and SOC3 account groups on JSS1.

The result was approximately a fourfold increase in scope from the original request and analytical support for more than $10 million of additional baseline effort.

02 · The Problem

When the Approved Baseline No Longer Matches the Work

The assignment began with a fairly simple question: were the existing SOC2 budgets sufficient to support the remaining project schedule?

Portions of the existing baseline had historically been underestimated. As the project progressed, a growing gap emerged between the approved baseline, the remaining schedule, and the amount of support effort realistically required to complete the work.

The objective therefore was not simply to extrapolate historical spending or apply a standard EAC equation.

I needed to determine what level of future effort was operationally reasonable given the remaining duration of the project, current staffing behavior, existing budgets, account maturity, and future resource requirements.

This made the work both a project-controls exercise and a workforce planning problem.

03 · Key Technical Insight

Recognizing That LOE Work Must Be Forecast Differently

Discrete Work

Performance Driven

Discrete work earns value through measurable physical accomplishment such as fabrication, installation, assembly, testing, or commissioning.

Future forecasts therefore have a strong relationship with productivity: how many hours are required to complete a measurable quantity of work?

Historical performance measures such as CPI can often provide meaningful information about expected future efficiency.

Level of Effort

Schedule Driven

Most SOC2 and SOC3 scope consisted of supervisory, project-support, and service functions that earn value primarily as time passes.

These resources are required because project work remains active, not because they manufacture a measurable quantity of physical product.

As a result, remaining schedule duration and future staffing requirements became more useful forecasting drivers than simply projecting historical CPI forward.

This distinction became one of my most important practical EVM lessons: the forecasting method should reflect the type of work being forecasted rather than applying one equation to every account.

04 · Developing the Methodology

Starting With Multiple Forecasting Signals

There was no established model for the assignment that I could simply update. I developed the initial methodology from the ground up and deliberately avoided relying on a single indicator.

My first approach considered three major forecasting signals:

Historical Performance

Reviewed total historical actuals and performance to understand how accurately each account had been planned over its lifetime.

Recent Performance

Examined recent labor burn and staffing behavior so that older project conditions did not dominate assumptions about how the account was currently operating.

Schedule Requirements

Evaluated forecast completion dates and remaining durations to determine how long supervisory and support resources would realistically need to remain active.

Accounting for Activity Maturity

I also considered whether the relative importance of these forecasting drivers should change as an account matured.

An account approaching completion contains substantial evidence about its actual execution, while an account that has only recently started has far less historical information available.

The early methodology therefore explored weighting forecasting signals differently based on account maturity rather than treating every activity identically.

05 · Building the Model

Understanding the Work Before Forecasting It

Historical Actuals

Reviewed historical labor consumption to understand how each account had behaved over time.

Existing Budgets

Compared approved baseline values with current expectations to identify potential planning gaps.

Remaining Budget

Evaluated how much approved effort remained relative to the remaining project schedule.

Burn Rates

Used recent labor consumption to understand present staffing levels and execution behavior.

Schedule Forecasts

Reviewed expected activity completion dates and remaining durations as major drivers of LOE demand.

Resource Loading

Examined planned resources and staffing requirements rather than treating forecast hours as an abstract financial value.

The spreadsheet was not the hardest part of the assignment. The difficult part was understanding what operational reality the spreadsheet was supposed to represent.

Building a defensible forecast required learning what the accounts actually did, why the resources were required, how long those requirements would persist, and whether the outputs made practical sense.

06 · Iterative Development

Challenging the Model Instead of Defending It

Future Figure: Forecasting Methodology Evolution

The methodology went through numerous review cycles rather than moving directly from initial analysis to implementation.

I repeatedly reviewed forecast outputs, assumptions, sensitivity to different drivers, and account-level reasonableness with my supervisor, members of Project Controls, project-management stakeholders, and PMO.

These discussions frequently focused less on whether a spreadsheet formula was mathematically correct and more on questions such as:

  • Why is this account behaving differently?
  • Does this forecast reflect the remaining schedule?
  • Is historical performance relevant to this type of work?
  • Are current staffing assumptions realistic?
  • What does EVM theory suggest?
  • Does the final answer make operational sense?

An important part of the process was being willing to change the methodology when the evidence showed that an earlier assumption was less appropriate.

07 · Final Forecasting Approach

Moving Toward a Schedule-Driven LOE Forecast

Forecasted Schedule

The future schedule became the primary foundation for determining how long support resources would be required.

Expected Duration

Remaining account duration was used to connect project timelines directly to required future effort.

Recent Burn Behavior

Current labor consumption helped establish realistic staffing levels without over-relying on older execution data.

Resource Requirements

Forecasts were evaluated against the actual people and functions required to support future project work.

As the work matured, the analysis demonstrated that schedule requirements should remain the dominant forecasting driver for these LOE accounts.

This did not make the earlier historical analysis unnecessary. Historical and recent performance remained useful validation tools for identifying unrealistic assumptions and understanding why prior budgets had differed from actual execution.

The final methodology therefore became less about identifying a single "winning formula" and more about using the appropriate forecasting logic for the type of work being analyzed.

08 · Stakeholder Engagement

Working Directly With PMO

This assignment also represented a change in the level of stakeholder responsibility I was given during the internship.

The project began with PMO directly inviting me into a meeting to discuss the rebaseline requirement rather than the assignment simply being passed through my supervisor.

Throughout development, I worked with PMO and Project Controls stakeholders to:

  • Understand the underlying planning requirement
  • Clarify account assumptions
  • Gather feedback on forecast outputs
  • Explain why different methodologies produced different results
  • Refine the model based on operational and EVM considerations
  • Present conclusions and planning recommendations

It was one of the first assignments where I moved beyond communicating results and began helping shape the planning recommendation itself.

10 · Beyond Cost Forecasting

The Assignment Became a Workforce Planning Exercise

Staffing Demand

Forecasting future LOE required understanding the level of supervision and project support needed as production activity changed over time.

Duration of Support

Staffing could not be considered independently from schedule duration because support functions remain necessary while underlying project work continues.

Future Labor Consumption

The resulting forecast connected project schedules and staffing assumptions to the expected future consumption of labor and budget.

The work ultimately sat at the intersection of finance, project controls, workforce planning, and operations rather than fitting cleanly into only one discipline.

11 · Ownership & Development

From Learning an Existing Process to Helping Develop One

Many of my early internship assignments involved learning established Seaspan processes and applying existing reporting structures.

This project was fundamentally different because there was no finished method waiting for me.

I had to interpret the problem, build the initial methodology, test different forecasting drivers, understand the underlying accounts, incorporate stakeholder feedback, revise assumptions, and eventually support recommendations with significant project consequences.

The work required technical analysis, EVM understanding, project knowledge, workforce reasoning, stakeholder engagement, and communication simultaneously.

More importantly, it demonstrated how project controls moves beyond measuring past performance and can directly influence what the future project plan should look like.

That transition from maintaining an existing system to helping develop a methodology became one of the strongest indicators of my growth during the internship.

12 · Key Lessons

What I Took From the Project

Methodology Must Match the Work

A forecasting equation is only useful when its assumptions correspond to how the underlying work is actually performed and measured.

Schedules Can Be Forecasting Inputs

For LOE support activities, remaining schedule duration can provide more useful information about future requirements than historical CPI alone.

Models Support Judgment

Analytical models should challenge and validate assumptions, not replace operational or managerial judgment.

Understand Before Calculating

The most difficult part of forecasting was rarely the math. It was understanding the project well enough to know which assumptions were reasonable.

Iteration Improves Analysis

The final method benefited from challenging earlier approaches and incorporating feedback rather than becoming attached to the first model.

Good Analysis Can Scale

The project's expansion from one account group to four showed how a useful analytical approach can move beyond its original problem and influence much broader decisions.
